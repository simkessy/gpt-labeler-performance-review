Why skip sampling and approval?

In the 2026 review data, 26,889 of 26,950 resolved reviews passed. Most approvals were automatic. Jobs with a numeric estimate took 6.6 hours on average from submission to automatic approval.

The approval decision was quick. The wait came before the review was created. The 6.6 hours includes other preparation. It is not all removable sample time.

What did the resource study find?

Among available histories, 21% of worker instances received no processing tasks. This is a worker count. It is not a percentage of money or processing hours wasted.

The target is about 3 h 9 min per job. Labeling and completion detection alone take 3 h 34 min. We must reduce that stage too.

Where do these assumptions come from?

The 3–5 hour range uses the observed multi-hour cost-estimation and pre-approval waits as context. It is a planning assumption, not a measured removable duration.

The 30–45 minute range targets part of the 73-minute wait after labels are saved. It keeps time for checks and final writing. No pilot has verified either range.

The baseline uses 756.8 minutes, the sum of the rounded stage measurements. Displayed totals are rounded to whole minutes.

What must the pilot prove?

Compare similar jobs. Measure time to usable output, failures, missing or duplicate labels, and actual cost. Keep failed and unfinished jobs in the results.

For the labeling stage, first separate provider completion time from the time our service notices it. Then test submission and completion checks. Keep label quality the same.

Study scope and evidence

The September census covers 3,727 user jobs. The timing chart uses 1,676 completed jobs with all required times. Completed jobs alone do not describe failure rates.

We selected 1,774 data-processing runs for detailed inspection. We retrieved 580 histories. Of these, 234 had positive worker-memory readings. No histories were available for September 1–7. These resource results describe the available sample, not every September job.

The approval rate uses resolved reviews from January through early October 2026. It includes automatic approvals. It is not a human-only approval rate.

Evidence was collected October 6–8. Outcomes were observed through October 7. Engineering work estimates exclude review and release lead time.
